SHOT 1 — DAY ABOVE, ANOTHER NIGHT BELOW. Wide, slightly elevated environmental view over a shallow shrinking jagüey, with broad dry ochre banks, in inland La Guajira. The remaining water forms an irregular low crescent across the lower-middle composition, surrounded by visibly dry banks. A small rancheria stands on the opposite bank: two low rectangular clay houses, a flat open enramada, a single empty ochre hammock beneath its shade. Three SMALL adult Wayuu people in loose full-length blue/terracotta clothing measure water near a plain clay jar; farther away two small paper goats cross a dry path. One branching multi-stem cardon at the near left edge and delicate sparse dry branches on the right provide framing and depth, not a cactus forest. Low layered clay hills and a few cut-paper clouds, sky confined to the upper quarter.

THE VISIBLE IMPOSSIBILITY: it is unmistakably warm DAY above the water, but the water reflects a vast deep INDIGO NIGHT, one crisp pale crescent moon and tiny stars which are ABSENT from the actual daytime sky. Reflected houses are discernible but their people are missing. The reflection behaves like another time, not a faithful mirror. Layered indigo paper extends into impossible depth inside an otherwise SHALLOW remaining sheet of water, while dry banks and ordinary people remain solid and sunlit. The near edge of this reflection is clear; thin turquoise paper ripple arcs break the moon gently. Compose this magical reflected world as a strong diagonal counterpart to the living rancheria, at least one third of the image devoted to its discovery. Still recognizable as scarce water, not a full lake, ocean or lush oasis. No infected water, corpses, levitation or black liquid leaving the pool. This is a visual foreshadowing of absence, not a literal epidemic mechanism.
